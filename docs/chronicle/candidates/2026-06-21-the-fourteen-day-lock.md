## The record

On 2026-06-21 the protocol meant to move its own inventory in a single afternoon: pause the second-generation sale engine, take back the unsold SYN it still held, and use that inventory to fund the third-generation engine. The origin's own ledger records the expectation: "Founder initially expected pause V2b, recover SYN, fund V3 immediately."
The contract refused the middle step. Its recovery function can pay only one destination — the vault address fixed into the contract at deployment. It can move only the entire remaining balance, never a part of it. Its sibling rescue function, the obvious side door, is forbidden from touching SYN at all. And unless the sale has concluded, the recovery cannot run until the engine has stood paused for a period compiled into the bytecode as a constant: fourteen days. The ledger states the four limits in one line: "recoverUnsoldSyn() is Vault-only, full balance only, rescue-protected, and timelocked unless concluded."
So the afternoon ran in the other order. At 14:47:06 UTC the third-generation engine was funded with 7,000,000 SYN sent from a separate wallet. At 14:55:24 UTC — eight minutes and eighteen seconds later — the second-generation engine was paused, still holding 6,989,000 SYN. The pause started the clock. The earliest second at which those tokens could move was 2026-07-05 at 14:55:24 UTC: fourteen days later, to the second.

## What the protocol did

It funded the new engine from elsewhere and wrote the constraint down instead of working around it. The ledger's fix line reads: "V3 was funded from a separate SYN wallet; V2b recovery was deferred and recorded." That day's readback log leaves the instruction in the imperative — "Do not call recoverUnsoldSyn() until a separate recovery ceremony is approved." — and the ledger leaves the rule it cost: "Never assume pause implies immediate recoverability."
Nobody could shorten the clock. The delay is a constant in the deployed contract, not a setting; the call reverts until it expires; and the only address it can ever pay is the vault the contract names. The wallet that signed the pause is the same wallet that owns the engine, and owning it bought no exemption.
The lock expired on 2026-07-05 at 14:55:24 UTC, and nothing happened that day. The recovery ran on 2026-07-10 at 08:51:24 UTC — four days, seventeen hours and fifty-six minutes after the earliest permitted moment, and just under nineteen days after the pause. It ran the only way the contract allows: one transaction, the entire remaining balance, 6,989,000 SYN, out of the paused engine and into the vault address written into it — the same vault the third-generation engine pays into. The engine's SYN balance reads zero today.
The origin's written record stops before this. Its repository, last committed on 2026-07-12, carries no note of the recovery: no transaction, no date, no report. This entry records the transaction, which is measurable. It does not claim that a recovery ceremony was approved beforehand, because that is not something the chain can show.

## Why this is recorded

Any protocol can say that nobody, its own builders included, can reach its inventory on a whim. The sentence is worth something only on a day when someone wants to.
On 2026-06-21 the protocol wanted its own unsold tokens back, for a purpose it considered good and urgent, and held the only key entitled to ask for them. The answer was no — not from a policy, a committee or a promise, but from a contract that had been deployed with the answer already inside it, and that went on saying no for fourteen days while the plan waited.
The delay changed nothing about where the tokens ended up: they went to the one address the bytecode had always named, in one movement, all of it. It changed only when — and it made the when public. A pause second anyone can read, a fixed period anyone can read, and a sum anyone can add. That is the difference between a rule a protocol follows and a rule it cannot break, and only the second kind is worth writing down.

Because isConcluded() is false, the timed path is the only path this recovery could have taken.
One read has moved since the day recorded here, and the second transaction is why.
